# comms-soul.md

How Intentional Trainwreck writes anything public: bios, captions, pitch emails,
video descriptions, ad copy, Reddit posts. The goal is to sound like a person wrote it,
because a person did. The list below is the stuff that makes text read as AI slop.
Avoid it.

## The hard rules (non-negotiable)

- **No em dashes (—) and no en dashes (–). Use a plain hyphen (-), a comma, or a period.**
  This is the single loudest AI tell in 2026. When you feel the urge for a dash, start
  a new sentence instead.
- **No semicolons as a fancy pause.** Two sentences, or a comma.
- **Vary sentence length on purpose.** A short punchy line next to a long one reads human.
  Every sentence the same length reads like a machine. ("Controlled chaos." next to a
  runner is the move.)
- **Be specific over impressive.** One real detail ("raised about $2.5k") beats ten
  adjectives.

## Sentence shapes to avoid

- "It's not X, it's Y." (the classic false-profundity reframe)
- "Not just X, but Y."
- "From X to Y." (sweeping range framing)
- "The kind of X that..."
- "Whether X or Y..."
- "It's a X. And that's okay."
- Rhetorical question, then you answer it.
- Stacking "more than" / "rather than" to fake insight.

## The rule of three

Stop grouping things in threes. "Rage, catharsis, and precision." "Fast, loud, and
technical." One tricolon is fine once. Two in a paragraph is a tell. Break them into
normal sentences.

## Opener crutches

Never start with: "In today's...", "In a world...", "Imagine...", "Picture this...",
"Ever wondered...", "As we head into 2026...", "Let's face it", "It's no secret".
Just start with the actual thing.

## Hedging (kills any voice)

Cut: "it's worth noting", "arguably", "it could be said", "in many cases", "generally
speaking", "to some extent", "that said", "potentially". Say it or don't.

## Transition / wrap-up cues

Cut: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "At the
end of the day", "That's the beauty of it", "when all is said and done".

## Grandiose nouns

Cut: tapestry, landscape, realm, testament, symphony, beacon, cornerstone, journey,
"world of", treasure trove, "ever-evolving", "timeless".

## Overused verbs

Cut: delve, leverage, harness, utilize, elevate, unlock, unleash, foster, navigate,
embark, "dive into", craft, curate, transform, revolutionize, streamline, spearhead.

## Empty intensifiers and filler

Cut: crucial, vital, essential, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changing, truly,
incredibly, remarkably, meticulous, holistic, synergy, paradigm, ecosystem,
multifaceted, plethora, myriad.

## Fake engagement

Cut: "Here's the thing", "Here's the kicker", "The best part?", "But wait", "Let's dive
in", "Buckle up", "spoiler".

## Music-bio clichés (the ones that actually threaten a band bio)

Cut: "burst onto the scene", "no strangers to", "sonic journey", "sonic landscape",
"hauntingly beautiful", "genre-defying", "seamlessly blends", "eclectic mix", "wall of
sound", "raw energy", "explosive", "boundary-pushing", "highly anticipated", "sure to
leave listeners wanting more", "unique sound", "a force to be reckoned with", "hailing
from".

Note: "eclectic" on its own is allowed here because it's genuinely how the band
describes itself. It's "eclectic MIX" (the stock phrase) that's banned.

## Do this instead

- Write the way you'd say it out loud to another musician.
- Name real bands, real numbers, real moments. Let one honest quote do the work of a
  paragraph of praise.
- Read it back. If it sounds like a press release wrote itself, tear out the fanciest
  sentence first.
- Leave a rough edge or two. Too-clean is its own tell.
